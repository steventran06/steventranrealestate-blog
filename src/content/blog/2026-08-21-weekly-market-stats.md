Across Greater Portland the single-family market is functioning with relatively limited supply: 6, 067 active single-family listings, 2.7 months of inventory and a 27.4% pending-to-active ratio. Average days on market for sold single-family homes is 43 days, which points to an active, faster-moving segment in many neighborhoods. Average single-family sale price in the aggregate is \$669, 612.

Condominiums are behaving differently. The metro condo pool shows about 1, 435 active listings and roughly 7 months of inventory, with average days on market for sold condos at 88 days. That points to more available choices and longer marketing times for many condo buyers and sellers compared with detached homes.

## 🔥 Where Single-Family Homes Look Most Competitive

When months of inventory is low and pending activity is high, listings typically move faster and competition increases. In Northeast Portland the single-family market sits at 1.6 months of inventory with a 35.2% pending-to-active ratio and sold homes averaging 29 days on market. North Portland and Southeast Portland show similarly tight conditions (1.7 and 2.0 months of inventory respectively), which often means buyers need to move quickly and sellers will see more interest in shorter windows.

## 🏢 The Condo Market Is a Different Story

Condos are acting differently across the metro. Several condo markets show multiple months of inventory and much longer days on market than nearby single-family segments. For example, North Portland condos have 8.6 months of inventory and sold properties averaged 111 days on market, and Lake Oswego/West Linn condos report about 10 months of inventory with average sold days on market near 96. That generally means more choices and slower-moving condo markets in many neighborhoods.

The contrast between condos and single-family homes is pronounced in several neighborhoods. In North Portland the single-family market is fast and tight (1.7 months, 31 days on market) while condos show 8.6 months of inventory and 111 days on market. Similar gaps appear in Beaverton and Northeast Portland. Practically, that means single-family buyers should expect more competition and faster timelines, while condo buyers will often find more inventory and longer decision windows. Sellers should match marketing and pricing strategies to which side of that gap their property falls on.

## 💰 What This Could Mean for Buyers

- If you’re buying a single-family home in neighborhoods like Northeast, North or Southeast Portland, expect a faster-moving process: properties are spending around 30 to 45 days on market and inventory is tight.
- Condo shoppers will often have more options and longer decision windows: many condo markets show 6 to 10+ months of inventory and significantly longer days on market.
- Look at areas labeled as buyer opportunities (Columbia County, Yamhill County, Marion County) if you want more negotiating room and time to inspect and compare homes.
- Bring financing and pre-approval documents up to date for single-family bids, since faster-moving listings will require quicker responses.
- For condos, prioritize comparing HOA budgets, reserves and recent assessment history, because you may have more time to shop between comparable units.

* * *

## 🏠 What This Could Mean for Sellers

- Sellers in low-inventory single-family neighborhoods can expect more interest and quicker timelines; prepare for multiple showings early and set a clear timeline for offers.
- Condo sellers in higher-inventory areas should build in more marketing time and be ready to demonstrate value through condition, staging and clear HOA documentation.
- Price and presentation matter across the board: in faster markets small price or condition advantages can speed a sale, while in slower markets strong presentation and realistic pricing reduce time on market.
- If your property falls in a buyer-opportunity area, plan for longer marketing and allow time for negotiations; flexibility on timing or small repairs may help the sale.

* * *

## Bottom Line

Portland is not one housing market. Inventory, demand and timelines vary by neighborhood, property type and price range, so buyers and sellers should focus on the specifics for their area and property.
