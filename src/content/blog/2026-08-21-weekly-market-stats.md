Across Greater Portland the market for single-family homes is noticeably quicker than the condo market. Metro single-family inventory sits at 2.7 months of inventory with about 27% of listings pending, and the average sold home spent 43 days on market. Those numbers point to a generally faster-moving market for detached homes in many parts of the metro.

Condos are behaving differently. The metro condo inventory is around 7 months of inventory, pending activity is lower (about 13%), and condos are taking longer to sell on average (88 days). That gap means buyers and sellers should approach condos with different expectations than they would for detached homes in the same neighborhoods.

## 🔥 Where Single-Family Homes Look Most Competitive

When you see single-family months of inventory down near 1.6 to 2.6 months, combined with pending-active ratios in the high 20s and 30s and short days on market, that typically means more competition and faster-moving sales. Northeast Portland (1.6 months, 35% pending, 29 days on market) and North Portland (1.7 months, 34% pending, 31 days) are good examples where buyers should expect quicker decision windows and sellers may see stronger demand.

## 🏢 The Condo Market Is a Different Story

Condos are acting differently in many parts of the metro: several condo markets have 5 to 10 months of inventory and much longer days on market than nearby single-family segments. Southeast Portland condos show about 4.8 months of inventory and 71 days on market, while Lake Oswego/West Linn condos are sitting at 10 months and about 96 days. That spread often reflects different buyer pools, price points and financing considerations for condos.

Comparing condos to single-family homes highlights consistent gaps. In North Portland, single-family inventory is 1.7 months while condos are 8.6 months, a 6.9-month gap; pending activity and days on market show the same split (single-family 34% pending and 31 DOM vs condos 14% pending and 111 DOM). Similar gaps appear in Beaverton and Lake Oswego. Practically, that means buyers generally have more time and choices when shopping for condos, while single-family buyers face tighter windows and more competition in the same neighborhoods.

## 💰 What This Could Mean for Buyers

- If you want more time to compare properties, look at condo markets where months of inventory and days on market are higher.
- In fast single-family pockets like Northeast and North Portland expect shorter decision timelines: be prepared with financing and a clear must-have list.
- Consider broadening search to nearby counties (Columbia, Yamhill, Marion) if you want more inventory and longer marketing times.
- Watch neighborhood-level indicators rather than metro averages; single-family and condo dynamics can be very different even within the same area.

* * *

## 🏠 What This Could Mean for Sellers

- Sellers of single-family homes in hot pockets should expect faster showings and less time on market; strong presentation and pricing alignment matter.
- Condo sellers may need longer marketing plans and targeted pricing, since condos are taking more time to sell and show lower pending activity.
- If you need to sell in a slower submarket, factor extra time for marketing and consider updates that make your listing stand out.
- Work with an agent who tracks neighborhood-level months of inventory and days on market so your strategy fits your property type and price range.

* * *

## Bottom Line

Portland is not one housing market: single-family and condo behavior can be very different by neighborhood and price range. Buyers and sellers should look at the specific area, property type and price band that matter to them before making decisions.
