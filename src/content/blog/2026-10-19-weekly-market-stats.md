Greater Portland is showing two distinct pictures: the single-family market is relatively tighter, with 3.3 months of inventory and roughly 49 days on market for sold homes, while the condo market has more supply and longer marketing times, with 7.8 months of inventory and about 104 days on market. Pending-to-active ratios back that up: single-family listings across the metro have a 23.1 pending-to-active ratio versus 11.8 for condos, which helps explain why homes and condos are behaving differently for buyers and sellers.

Examples here include Northeast Portland (2.1 months, 32 days on market) and North Portland (1.9 months, 42 days). In those areas, buyers have less time to decide and there are fewer choices on market.

## 🏢 The Condo Market Is a Different Story

Condos around the metro are generally behaving differently than detached homes. The overall condo market sits at 7.8 months of inventory and about 104 days on market, and many condo submarkets show long marketing times (for example, Gresham/Troutdale condos average 121 days on market). Pending activity for condos is lower in most areas, so sellers may need more time and buyers can expect more choices.

Compare single-family vs condos in the same neighborhoods and the contrasts are clear. In North Portland single-family inventory is 1.9 months while condos are 8.8 months, pending ratios are 28.9 vs 6.7, and median days on market are 42 vs 126. Northeast Portland shows a similar pattern: 2.1 months and 32 days for single-family vs 8.4 months and 132 days for condos. In short, condos tend to offer more inventory, lower pending activity and longer marketing windows than detached homes across many parts of the metro.

## 💰 What This Could Mean for Buyers

- If you’re shopping single-family homes in North or Northeast Portland, be prepared to move quickly: low inventory and short days on market mean limited time to make decisions.
- Consider condo markets or higher-inventory neighborhoods (Yamhill County, Columbia County) if you want more choices and a slower pace to compare properties.
- Get your financing and inspections lined up early so you can act confidently in faster-moving single-family areas without sacrificing due diligence.
- Use longer condo marketing times to your advantage: inspect comparable sales and condition carefully, and you may have more room to negotiate on price or repairs.
- Work with an agent who knows the specific neighborhood and property type, citywide averages hide big local differences.

* * *

## 🏠 What This Could Mean for Sellers

- Sellers in the hottest single-family pockets (North, Northeast, Southeast Portland) can expect more buyer interest; price and presentation will matter for standing out.
- Condo sellers should plan for longer marketing times and lower pending activity; realistic pricing and strong photographs/marketing help shorten time on market.
- In higher-inventory single-family areas like Yamhill or Columbia counties, expect buyers to have more choices, emphasize condition, disclosure clarity and responsiveness.
- Staging and small, cost-effective updates can make a bigger difference where buyers can shop more (markets with 4+ months of inventory).
- Talk with your agent about comparable active inventory and typical days on market in your neighborhood before setting timing or price expectations.

* * *

## Bottom Line

Portland is not one housing market. Buyers and sellers should evaluate the specific neighborhood, property type and price range they care about rather than leaning on broad averages.
