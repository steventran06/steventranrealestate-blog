This report looks back at August 2026 and highlights the data points that may matter for buyers as we head into September. Ten weekly market snapshots were available for the month, giving a full-picture view across Portland Metro neighborhoods.

The broad takeaway: supply moved modestly in different directions depending on neighborhood. Some areas showed more homes and slower buyer activity, while others stayed tight with homes selling quickly. Price reductions were common enough that buyers should watch recently reduced listings as part of their search.

> **About this report:** I reviewed 10 weekly Portland Metro market reports from August 2026 to see what changed and what those signals could mean for buyers this month.

* * *

## 🏡 Where Buyers Had More Options

Buyers had more choices in several places in August. Greater Portland single-family active listings rose by 67 and the Tigard/Tualatin/Sherwood/Wilsonville area added about 49 single-family listings, giving shoppers more to consider. Condo buyers also saw larger inventories in parts of the region, especially Northeast Portland where condo listings increased noticeably.

### Columbia County

**217 active listings | about 73 days to sell**

Homes were also taking longer to sell than in many parts of the metro, which can give buyers more time to compare properties and potentially negotiate.

### Yamhill County

**418 active listings | about 62 days to sell**

Homes were also taking longer to sell than in many parts of the metro, which can give buyers more time to compare properties and potentially negotiate.

### Marion County

**646 active listings | about 50 days to sell**

Homes were taking a little longer to sell, which can reduce some of the pressure to make an immediate decision.

### West Portland

**542 active listings | about 38 days to sell**

This area gave buyers comparatively more breathing room than the fastest-moving parts of the Portland Metro.

* * *

## 🔥 Where Homes Were Moving Faster

Some neighborhoods remained competitive and gave buyers less time to decide. Northeast Portland single-family homes were still moving quickly, selling in roughly 29 days on average, and Lake Oswego/West Linn single-family listings dropped sharply, with 39 fewer active listings during the month. In parts of North Portland condos, active listings fell while buyer activity picked up.

### Northeast Portland

**297 active listings | about 29 days to sell**

Homes were selling relatively quickly here, averaging about 29 days. Buyers interested in well-priced properties may need to be ready to make decisions faster than they would in slower-moving areas.

### North Portland

**142 active listings | about 33 days to sell**

Homes were selling in about 33 days on average, keeping this among the faster-moving markets in the report.

### Southeast Portland

**455 active listings | about 33 days to sell**

Homes were selling in about 33 days on average, keeping this among the faster-moving markets in the report.

### Tigard, Tualatin, Sherwood and Wilsonville

**511 active listings | about 48 days to sell**

This area remained more competitive than the markets offering buyers the most time and choice.

## 🏢 Houses vs. Condos

Condo markets behaved differently from detached homes in August. In a few areas, condo listings increased enough to give buyers more choices and bargaining room, while other condo pockets showed faster sale times and fewer options.

For buyers, that means you may find more selection and a bit more time in some condo neighborhoods, but you should still check fees, rules and condition carefully, those factors matter as much as price when comparing condos to houses.

The difference is especially noticeable in Northeast Portland. Houses were taking about 29 days to sell, compared with about 118 days for condos.

## 📈 What Changed During August?

Across Portland Metro in August, single-family listings rose in several places but buyer activity measured by pending signs generally eased in many markets. Average sale prices were largely steady with small declines in some neighborhoods; most price changes were modest rather than dramatic. Condo supply grew in parts of Northeast Portland and Lake Oswego, while pockets like North Portland and Lake Oswego saw faster buyer interest despite lower active listings in other segments.

- Tigard, Tualatin, Sherwood and Wilsonville: active listings increased by 49.
- Lake Oswego/West Linn: there were 39 fewer active listings.
- Milwaukie/Clackamas: active listings increased by 27.

## 💰 Where Sellers Were Cutting Prices

There were 159 unique properties tracked with price changes in August and a typical reduction was \$54,000, which equaled about a 6.25% cut for the median reduced listing. Price cuts can mean a seller is more motivated or simply that the original list price was optimistic. A reduction is a negotiating clue, but it does not automatically mean a property is a bargain, condition, location and comparables still matter.

## 💡 What This Could Mean If You're Buying in September

- Expect a bit more selection among single-family homes in parts of the region; compare multiple listings quickly so you do not miss good fits.
- If a neighborhood has very few homes for sale and short average sell times, be ready to make decisions faster but keep inspections and financing protections in place.
- Watch reduced listings, the median reduction in August was about \$54,000 (roughly 6.25%), and use reductions as a starting point for negotiation, not proof of value.
- For condo shoppers, look for areas where inventory increased (for example, Northeast Portland) to get more choices, but always factor in HOA fees and building condition.

* * *

## 👀 What I'm Watching in September

- See if Greater Portland single-family listings continue to climb after a 67-listing increase in August.
- Watch Northeast Portland supply and sale pace, single-family homes were selling in about 29 days with very limited availability.
- Track price reductions in neighborhoods showing the most cuts (for example HOSFORD FARMS TERRA, FIRST ADDITION and RALEIGH HILLS) to spot potential negotiation opportunities.

## Bottom Line

Portland Metro includes many different local markets; real opportunities depend on exact location, property type, condition and price range, so local context matters for every buyer.

Market statistics are based on the available weekly TMO Reports and historical price-drop reports analyzed for August 2026. Market conditions, listing availability and pricing are subject to change and can vary significantly by neighborhood, property type and price range.
