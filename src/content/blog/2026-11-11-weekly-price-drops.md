## What Buyers Should Know About Price Reductions

A price reduction is one data point. Buyers should compare recent comparable sales and current active listings, assess property condition through inspections, and factor likely repair or renovation costs. Consider financing terms, appraisal risk and how the price fits your lender’s valuation. Also evaluate local demand and time on market; a reduction can reflect buyer leverage, seller motivation, or simply a re-pricing to current neighborhood comps.

A price reduction can indicate seller motivation, but it can also mean that the original asking price was higher than the market supported.

The reduction itself is only one piece of the puzzle. Buyers should still review comparable sales, property condition, disclosures, inspection findings, financing terms and neighborhood demand before deciding how much value a price change actually creates.
