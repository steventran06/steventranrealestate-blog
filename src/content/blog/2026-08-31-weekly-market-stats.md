## 🏢 The Condo Market Is a Different Story

Condos are behaving differently in many parts of the metro. At the aggregate level Greater Portland condominiums show about 7.1 months of inventory, a pending-to-active ratio near 13, and an average days on market around 86. Several condo submarkets have even higher inventory: Lake Oswego/West Linn condos sit at 9.9 months, and North Portland condos at 8.8 months.

| Area | Single-Family Inventory | Condo Inventory | Inventory Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| North Portland | 1.8 months | 8.8 months | 7 months |
| Northeast Portland | 1.6 months | 8.4 months | 6.8 months |
| Lake Oswego/West Linn | 3.5 months | 9.9 months | 6.4 months |
| Beaverton | 2.6 months | 8.1 months | 5.5 months |
| West Portland | 3.4 months | 7.3 months | 3.9 months |

Across multiple neighborhoods the gap between single-family and condo markets is large. In Northeast Portland single-family inventory is 1.6 months versus 8.4 months for condos, and single-family homes sell in about 29 days while condos average 118 days. North Portland shows a 7-month inventory gap (1.8 months single-family vs 8.8 months condos) and much longer condo marketing times. In short, single-family homes generally have lower inventory and move faster; condos tend to offer more choices and longer marketing windows.

## 💰 What This Could Mean for Buyers

- If you’re shopping for a single-family home in the hottest pockets (Northeast, North, Southeast), be prepared to move quickly: fewer listings and shorter days on market mean decisions happen faster.
- In higher-inventory areas (Columbia County, Marion County, Yamhill County) you’ll likely have more time to inspect, compare and negotiate.
- Condo buyers should expect more options but also longer marketing times, plan for a slower pace and look for value in areas with high condo inventory like Lake Oswego/West Linn and North Portland.
- Have financing and contingencies in order before writing an offer in fast-moving single-family neighborhoods; in slower markets you can prioritize inspections and timelines that matter to you.

* * *

## 🏠 What This Could Mean for Sellers

- Sellers in the tight single-family pockets can expect a faster pace; prioritize strong pricing, excellent marketing and quick availability for showings.
- For condo sellers, higher months of inventory and longer days on market mean presentation and pricing matter, clear photos, minor updates and realistic pricing can shorten time to sale.
- If your property is in a slower county-level market, be ready to allow a longer marketing period and consider targeted improvements that differentiate the listing.
- Regardless of market speed, work with your agent on comparable sales and a plan for timing, since single-family and condo markets are moving differently across neighborhoods.

* * *

## Bottom Line

Portland is not a single housing market. Conditions vary by neighborhood, property type and price range, so buyers and sellers should look at the specific area and property class they care about.
