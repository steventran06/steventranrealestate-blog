This article looks back at what happened in the Portland Metro housing market in September and highlights a few signals that could matter for buyers in October. Four weekly market snapshots were available for the month.

> **About this report:** I reviewed 4 weekly Portland Metro market reports from September 2026 to see what changed and what those signals could mean for buyers this month.

* * *

## 🏡 Where Buyers Had More Options

In September many areas saw modest increases in active listings, which generally gave buyers a bit more choice and, in some neighborhoods, more time to consider options or negotiate.

### Columbia County

**235 active listings | about 75 days to sell**

Homes were also taking longer to sell than in many parts of the metro, which can give buyers more time to compare properties and potentially negotiate.

### Yamhill County

**418 active listings | about 60 days to sell**

Homes were also taking longer to sell than in many parts of the metro, which can give buyers more time to compare properties and potentially negotiate.

### Marion County

**650 active listings | about 53 days to sell**

Homes were taking a little longer to sell, which can reduce some of the pressure to make an immediate decision.

### Oregon City/Canby

**378 active listings | about 54 days to sell**

There were 21 more homes for sale in the latest report than in the earlier snapshot. Homes were taking a little longer to sell, which can reduce some of the pressure to make an immediate decision.

## 🏢 Houses vs. Condos

Condo markets in September showed different behavior than detached homes. Several condo areas saw average days to sell rise, suggesting buyers had a little more time in some pockets. That can open room to compare listings and recent sales before making an offer.

By contrast, some single-family neighborhoods continued to sell faster, so timing and readiness remain important for buyers seeking detached homes.

The difference is especially noticeable in Northeast Portland. Houses were taking about 32 days to sell, compared with about 124 days for condos.

## 📈 What Changed During September?

Across the region single-family active listings rose in a number of areas, Greater Portland single-family listings increased by 181 to 6,201 while average sale price stayed roughly flat near \$666,000. That combination suggests slightly more choices without a major price shift. Condo markets were mixed: Lake Oswego/West Linn condos showed high listing counts and inventory near 10.2, while several condo neighborhoods recorded longer average days to sell, with increases of 20 days or more in a few places. Buyer activity for single-family homes ticked down in multiple markets, but some neighborhoods, like Northeast Portland, still showed relatively short times to sell, near the low 30s in days.

- Southeast Portland: active listings increased by 50.
- West Portland: active listings increased by 23.
- Oregon City/Canby: active listings increased by 21.
- Lake Oswego/West Linn: active listings increased by 21.

## 💰 Where Sellers Were Cutting Prices

There were 379 unique listings tracked for price changes in September and 370 had known reductions. The typical reduction was about \$51,000, or roughly 6.5 percent. Price cuts can reflect seller motivation or adjustments to an initial asking price, and they give buyers useful context, but a reduction by itself does not automatically make a property a bargain.

## 💡 What This Could Mean If You're Buying in October

- Expect a bit more choice in several single-family neighborhoods, more listings appeared in September but prices stayed fairly steady.
- Watch condo listings closely; many condo markets showed longer average days to sell and typical price reductions around \$51,000, so buyers may find more room to compare and ask questions.
- If you are targeting low-inventory, fast-moving areas (for example parts of Northeast Portland), be prepared to act quickly and have documents ready.
- Use recent price reductions as a signal to investigate further, but confirm condition and comparables before assuming a reduced price equals value.

* * *

## 👀 What I'm Watching in October

- How October listings change in areas with the biggest inventory increases in September, such as Southeast Portland and Columbia County.
- Price reductions in hotspots named in the September report (Hillsdale, Hosford Farms Terra, Portland Heights, Raleigh Hills and Sylvan Highlands).
- Whether condo average days to sell keep rising or begin to shorten again, which would affect how much time buyers have to decide.

## Bottom Line

Portland Metro is made up of many local markets. Opportunities depend on location, property type, condition and price range, so local details matter when planning your next move.
