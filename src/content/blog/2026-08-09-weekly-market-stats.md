Across Greater Portland the single-family market shows relatively tight supply: 2.5 months of inventory, about 28% of active listings under contract, and sold homes averaging 43 days on market. That mix points to a market where many detached homes are still moving in a matter of weeks for buyers who are ready to act.

Condominiums are behaving very differently. At the metro level condos sit at 6.9 months of inventory, just under 14% of active listings are pending, and sold condos averaged 85 days on market. That creates more choices and generally longer marketing times for condo sellers and buyers in many neighborhoods.

## 🏢 The Condo Market Is a Different Story

Condos across the metro are showing higher inventory and slower turnover than detached homes. Metro condo inventory is 6.9 months, pending-to-active ratio 13.9%, and average days on market 85. Certain condo submarkets, like North Portland, have nearly 10 months of inventory and very long average marketing times, so condos are often a different playbook than single-family homes.

| Area | Single-Family Inventory | Condo Inventory | Inventory Gap |

| NW Portland | 3 months | 7.9 months | 4.9 months |

The contrast between single-family and condos is stark in many neighborhoods. North Portland single-family inventory is 1.6 months versus 9.8 months for condos, with single-family homes averaging 29 days on market compared with 112 for condos. Similar gaps appear in Lake Oswego/West Linn, Beaverton, NW Portland and others. That means strategies that work for detached homes, quick pricing, rapid showings, may not translate to condo listings that need longer exposure and different positioning.

* * *

## 💰 What This Could Mean for Buyers

- If you’re looking in Northeast, North or Southeast Portland expect faster timelines: many single-family listings are under contract in roughly 4 weeks, so have financing and inspections lined up.
- For condos, especially in North Portland and other high-inventory areas, expect longer marketing times and more negotiating room, you’ll have more choices and time to compare.
- In outlying counties like Columbia, Yamhill and Marion, higher months of inventory and longer DOM mean buyers can be more selective and may have more leverage on terms and timing.
- When shopping higher-priced submarkets like Lake Oswego/West Linn, note that single-family inventory is higher than the city hot spots but prices are substantially higher, so balance location and budget accordingly.

* * *

## 🏠 What This Could Mean for Sellers

- Sellers in the fastest single-family neighborhoods should price competitively and be ready for quick showings: low inventory and higher pending ratios mean less marketing time.
- Condo sellers should plan for longer exposure: pricing, condition and marketing matter more when the typical condo sells in multiple months rather than weeks.
- If you’re listing in county markets with higher inventory, build in time for offers and consider flexible timelines, homes there average several weeks to months on market.
- Across the board, clear condition and professional photos help in both quick and slower markets; faster markets reward readiness, slower markets reward standout presentation.

* * *

## Bottom Line

Portland is not one housing market. Single-family and condo dynamics vary a lot by neighborhood and price point, so buyers and sellers should look at the specific area, property type and price range that matters to them.
