Greater Portland overall shows relatively low months of inventory for single-family homes, with 2.5 months of inventory across the metro and an average days on market of 43 for sold single-family properties. That level of inventory typically points to a market where homes move at a steady clip and competition exists in many price bands, especially inside the city.

Condominiums are behaving differently: the metro condo market has 6.9 months of inventory and average days on market of 85. That gap between detached homes and condos means buyers and sellers should expect distinct dynamics depending on property type and neighborhood.

## 🔥 Where Single-Family Homes Look Most Competitive

Where single-family listings are most competitive, months of inventory are very low and pending activity is high. For example, Northeast Portland sits at 1.5 months of inventory with a pending-to-active ratio above 37 percent and sold homes averaging about 28 days on market. Low inventory, strong pending activity and short marketing times usually mean faster-moving showings and less time for buyers to deliberate.

## 🏢 The Condo Market Is a Different Story

Condo markets across the metro are generally slower-moving than detached homes right now. Several condo submarkets show five to nearly ten months of inventory, and sold condos often sit on the market for multiple months. That creates different expectations for pricing traction and marketing timelines compared with single-family homes.

| Area | Single-Family Inventory | Condo Inventory | Inventory Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| North Portland | 1.6 months | 9.8 months | 8.2 months |
| Lake Oswego/West Linn | 3.8 months | 9.3 months | 5.5 months |
| Beaverton | 2.7 months | 8.1 months | 5.4 months |
| NW Portland | 3 months | 7.9 months | 4.9 months |
| Northeast Portland | 1.5 months | 6.3 months | 4.8 months |

The split between condos and single-family homes is pronounced in multiple neighborhoods. In North Portland, single-family homes show about 1.6 months of inventory and roughly 29 days on market, while condos sit at 9.8 months of inventory and about 112 days on market. Similar gaps appear in Lake Oswego/West Linn and NW Portland. In short, condos are offering more buyer leverage and more time on market in many areas, while detached homes remain comparatively faster-moving.

## 💰 What This Could Mean for Buyers

- If you are shopping for a single-family home in hot pockets like Northeast, North or Southeast Portland, expect listings to go under contract quickly and be prepared with financing and a clear decision timeline.
- On condo hunts, especially in North Portland, Lake Oswego/West Linn and Beaverton, you can expect longer marketing times and more inventory to choose from, which often translates into more flexibility on price and terms.
- In outlying counties such as Columbia, Yamhill and Marion, higher months of inventory and longer days on market mean you can take more time to compare options and inspect value versus needed repairs.
- Work with your agent to size offers around local days on market and pending activity rather than broad metro headlines; property type and neighborhood matter a lot.
- If timing is important, ask for up-to-date pending-to-active ratios and days on market in the specific price range you care about before writing an offer.

* * *

## 🏠 What This Could Mean for Sellers

- Sellers in the most competitive single-family neighborhoods should plan for quicker showings and shorter marketing windows; prioritize curb appeal and staging to capture early interest.
- For condo sellers, expect a longer sales timeline; accurate pricing and clear marketing about HOA fees and amenities become more important when buyers have time to compare.
- In markets with higher months of inventory, be prepared to discuss negotiated timing and terms with buyers and consider flexible closing windows to attract offers.
- Use local comparables from your neighborhood and property type. Metro averages hide important differences between areas like Northeast Portland and rural counties.
- Work with your agent on a plan for pricing, marketing cadence and showing availability that matches how quickly or slowly your specific submarket is moving.

* * *

## Bottom Line

Portland is not one housing market. Single-family homes and condos are moving at different speeds across neighborhoods and counties, so buyers and sellers should evaluate their specific neighborhood, property type and price range for the most useful picture.
