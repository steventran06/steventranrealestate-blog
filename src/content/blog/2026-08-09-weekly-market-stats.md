Greater Portland is showing a clear split between detached homes and condo inventory. Across the metro, single-family listings sit at about 2.5 months of inventory with homes sold in roughly 43 days and an average sale price near \$671, 320. That overall picture points to relatively limited supply for detached homes, and buyers should expect less time to consider offers in the busiest neighborhoods.

Condominiums are behaving differently. Metro condos show about 6.9 months of inventory and an average days-on-market near 85 with an average sale price around \$364, 792. That higher inventory and longer marketing time means many condo shoppers currently have more choices and flexibility than buyers of detached homes.

## 🔥 Where Single-Family Homes Look Most Competitive

The most competitive single-family pockets are concentrated inside the city. Northeast, North and Southeast Portland are showing low months of inventory (about 1.5 to 1.9 months), strong pending activity and short marketing times (around 28 to 29 days). Low inventory, high pending activity and shorter days on market generally indicate faster-moving listing windows and more competition among buyers. If you’re looking in these neighborhoods expect the process to move quickly and plan accordingly.

## 🏢 The Condo Market Is a Different Story

Condos are acting like a different market in many places. Several condo areas have months of inventory in the mid-to-high single digits, and some locations push into the high single digits. For example, North Portland condos show about 9.8 months of inventory and average days on market over 110. That means condo shoppers can often take more time to compare options, while condo sellers face a longer marketing cycle than most detached-home sellers.

Comparing condos to single-family homes highlights major gaps. In North Portland there is an 8.2-month inventory gap: single-family inventory sits around 1.6 months versus 9.8 months for condos, and average days on market are roughly 29 versus 112. Similar gaps show up in Lake Oswego, Beaverton, NW and other areas where condo inventory and days on market are meaningfully higher. In short, single-family listings in many neighborhoods are faster-moving and tighter, while condos generally offer more supply and slower marketing timelines.

## 💰 What This Could Mean for Buyers

- If you’re shopping single-family homes in Northeast, North or Southeast Portland expect a faster timeline: roughly 1.5 to 1.9 months of inventory and homes selling in about 4 weeks.
- Condo buyers should expect more choices and time: metro condos average about 6.9 months of inventory and roughly 85 days on market; in North Portland condos are much slower (about 9.8 months, 112 days).
- Look beyond central neighborhoods for more negotiating room: Columbia County (4.4 months, about 73 days) and Yamhill/Marion counties (around 4.2 months) typically give buyers more flexibility.
- Know the price landscape: metro single-family average sale price is about \$671, 320, while metro condo average sale price is about \$364, 792, factor that into affordability and mortgage planning.
- When comparing options, match your timeline and financing readiness to each submarket’s speed; tighter markets require quicker decisions, slower markets allow more time.

* * *

## 🏠 What This Could Mean for Sellers

- Sellers of single-family homes in tight neighborhoods (Northeast, North, Southeast) should be prepared for a shorter marketing window and strong buyer interest; time listings and showings accordingly.
- Condo sellers face longer marketing cycles on average; pricing, condition and presentation tend to matter more when inventory and days on market are higher.
- In higher-inventory counties like Columbia, Yamhill and Marion, realistic pricing and a clear marketing plan are important because homes typically stay on market longer.
- Use local comps and days-on-market data for your specific neighborhood and property type; metro averages hide meaningful variation by area and product.
- Work with your agent to set a timeline that reflects your area’s months of inventory and expected days on market rather than broad citywide assumptions.

* * *

## Bottom Line

Portland is not one housing market, neighborhood, property type and price range matter, so buyers and sellers should review the specific numbers for their area and property type before making decisions.

Market statistics are based on the weekly TMO Reports data used for this analysis and are subject to change. Real estate conditions can vary significantly by neighborhood, property type and price range.
