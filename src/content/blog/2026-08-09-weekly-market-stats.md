Across Greater Portland the single-family segment looks relatively tight: 2.5 months of inventory, a 28.3 pending-to-active ratio, and homes that sold in about 43 days on average. There are nearly 6, 000 active single-family listings regionwide and the average sale price sits around \$671, 320, so many buyers will still find competition in popular neighborhoods.

Condominiums are behaving differently: the metro condo pool shows about 6.9 months of inventory, a much lower pending-to-active ratio of 13.9, and average days on market around 85. With roughly 1, 400 condo listings and an average condo sale near \$364, 792, condo buyers generally see more choices and longer marketing times than for detached homes in many areas.

## 🔥 Where Single-Family Homes Look Most Competitive

Certain single-family neighborhoods are noticeably faster-moving. Northeast, North and Southeast Portland all show very low months of inventory (1.5 to 1.9 months), high pending-to-active ratios (around the mid-30s for the top spots) and sold homes closing in the high 20s for days on market. Low inventory plus brisk pending activity and short marketing times usually means more competition and quicker decision-making for buyers, and less time on market for sellers.

Higher inventory and longer marketing windows can create more flexibility for buyers on timing, inspections and negotiating terms, and more time to comparison-shop.

## 🏢 The Condo Market Is a Different Story

Condos are not following the same pattern as detached homes in many parts of the metro. Several condo markets show substantially higher months of inventory and much longer days on market, for example North Portland condos sit near 9.8 months with average days on market over 110. That means condo buyers often have more options and more time to evaluate deals, while condo sellers face a different competitive set than single-family sellers.

The gap between single-family and condo dynamics is stark in places. In North Portland single-family inventory is about 1.6 months versus 9.8 months for condos, and average days on market are roughly 29 for houses but 112 for condos. Northeast Portland shows a similar split: 1.5 months for single-family versus 6.3 months for condos, and 28 days on market for houses versus 113 for condos. Treating property type separately matters: pricing, marketing and expected timelines can vary dramatically between condos and detached homes even inside the same neighborhood.

## 💰 What This Could Mean for Buyers

- If you’re shopping for a single-family home in the hottest pockets (Northeast, North, Southeast), expect fast-moving listings and be prepared to move quickly on homes you like.
- Condo shoppers in many areas have more choices and time: markets like North Portland show nearly 10 months of condo inventory and long marketing times, which can give buyers more leverage on terms and timing.
- Look beyond city-level headlines and compare months of inventory and days on market for your target neighborhood and property type before making offers.
- Consider financing and inspection timelines that match market speed: faster single-family markets reward decisive, well-prepared buyers; slower markets give room to negotiate and inspect.

* * *

## 🏠 What This Could Mean for Sellers

- Sellers in the most competitive single-family neighborhoods should price and prepare listings for quick exposure; homes there are moving in the high 20s to 40 days range.
- Condo sellers should plan for a different cadence: many condo markets show longer days on market and higher inventory, so expect a longer marketing period and emphasize condition, styling and clear HOA information.
- If you’re listing in slower single-family markets (Columbia, Yamhill, Marion), you have more time to make improvements and stage, but still price to the local demand level.
- Work with your agent to align timing and expectations to your specific price range and neighborhood rather than relying on broad metro averages.

* * *

## Bottom Line

Portland is not one housing market. Buyers and sellers should look at their specific neighborhood, property type and price range to make the best decisions.

Market statistics are based on the weekly TMO Reports data used for this analysis and are subject to change. Real estate conditions can vary significantly by neighborhood, property type and price range.
