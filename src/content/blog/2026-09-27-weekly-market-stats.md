Across Greater Portland the single-family market looks relatively tight: 6, 201 active single-family listings with about 2.9 months of inventory and an average 45 days on market for sold homes. That mix tends to produce faster-moving deals in neighborhoods where inventory is lowest and buyer interest is concentrated.
Condominiums are behaving differently. There are 1, 440 active condo listings and roughly 7.5 months of inventory, with condos averaging about 95 days on market for sold units. That gap means condos generally give buyers more time to evaluate options and may require a different selling approach than detached homes.

## 🏢 The Condo Market Is a Different Story

Condo markets are acting differently than detached homes across many parts of the metro. On average condos have higher months of inventory and longer days on market, and pending-to-active ratios are lower. That translates into more choices and more time for condo buyers, and it also requires condo sellers to be strategic on pricing and presentation.

The differences are stark in several neighborhoods. In Northeast Portland the inventory gap between single-family homes and condos is 7.2 months, and condos sit on the market about 92 days longer (124 days vs 32). Other areas show similar patterns: condos typically have multiple months more inventory, substantially lower pending activity and longer average marketing times than nearby single-family homes. That means the playbook for buyers and sellers should vary by property type, even within the same neighborhood.

## 💰 What This Could Mean for Buyers

- If you’re shopping single-family homes in tight pockets like Northeast or North Portland, expect faster timelines and more competition; be prepared to move quickly when the right house appears.
- Buyers looking for flexibility should consider markets with higher months of inventory such as Columbia County or Yamhill County, where homes spend more time on market and there may be more room to negotiate contract terms.
- Condo shoppers can often take more time to compare options, especially in Lake Oswego/West Linn, Northeast Portland condos, and Beaverton where condo inventory is comparatively high.
- Don’t assume every neighborhood behaves the same: compare months of inventory and days on market for your specific area and property type before making an offer.

* * *

## 🏠 What This Could Mean for Sellers

- Sellers of single-family homes in hot pockets should expect quicker showings and shorter decision windows; aim for clean presentation and clear pricing to capture buyer interest.
- Condo sellers will likely need a more tailored plan: competitive pricing, strong marketing and attention to condo-specific issues like dues and reserve disclosures, since condos are taking longer to sell.
- If you’re selling in an area with higher months of inventory, be realistic about timing and consider incentives that make your listing stand out without overpricing.
- Work with an agent who looks at local, property-type data, months of inventory and days on market vary widely by neighborhood and structure, and those numbers should guide your strategy.

* * *

## Bottom Line

Portland is not one housing market. Conditions vary by neighborhood, property type and price range, so buyers and sellers should evaluate the specific area and property class they care about before making decisions.
