Across Greater Portland single-family homes show about 2.5 months of inventory, with an average 43 days on market for sold homes and a pending-to-active ratio near 29 percent. Those numbers point to a market where detached homes are relatively tighter and move faster than the condo inventory.
Condominiums look different: the metro condo inventory is roughly 7.1 months, average days on market sits near 87, and the pending-to-active ratio is lower. In other words, condos generally offer more choices and longer marketing windows in many parts of the metro.

## 🔥 Where Single-Family Homes Look Most Competitive

The most competitive single-family pockets are places like Northeast and North Portland, where inventory sits around 1.5 to 1.6 months and days on market for sold homes are under 30 days. Low inventory, strong pending activity and short marketing times usually mean homes sell faster and buyers have less time to decide, those neighborhoods are simply more competitive right now.

## 🏢 The Condo Market Is a Different Story

Condos are behaving differently from detached homes in many areas. Several condo markets show five months of inventory or more, and some submarkets, like Lake Oswego, have double-digit months of inventory for condos. That means condo buyers often have more time to compare options, and condo listings generally take longer to sell.

Across multiple submarkets the gap between single-family and condo conditions is striking. For example, North Portland shows about 1.6 months of inventory for single-family homes versus 9.5 months for condos, and sold single-family homes close in about 29 days compared with 112 days for condos. Similar gaps appear in Lake Oswego and Northeast Portland. Put simply, single-family homes are the faster-moving, more competitive segment in many neighborhoods, while condos are offering more inventory and longer marketing windows.

## 💰 What This Could Mean for Buyers

- If you’re shopping for a single-family home in hot pockets like Northeast or North Portland, expect faster timelines and plan to be ready with financing and a clear decision process.
- Condo shoppers can take advantage of longer marketing times and larger inventories in many areas, which allows more time to compare properties and review condo documents.
- Look at the specific neighborhood and property type: a single-family home and a condo in the same zip code can behave very differently, so don’t generalize across the whole metro.
- In higher-inventory areas (Columbia County, Yamhill, Marion), buyers will typically find more options and more time to negotiate on terms and contingencies.
- Work with an agent who watches months of inventory, pending activity and days on market by neighborhood and price range, not just metro averages.

* * *

## 🏠 What This Could Mean for Sellers

- Sellers in fast-moving single-family pockets should expect shorter marketing windows and stronger competition among buyers; preparing the home and pricing realistically matters.
- Condo sellers should be prepared for longer marketing times and may need to focus on pricing, condition and presentation to stand out.
- Price and timing matter by submarket: a strategy that works in Northeast Portland won’t necessarily fit Lake Oswego or Columbia County, know your local numbers.
- If you need a quick sale in a slower market, be clear about timing and consider adjustments to price or terms to attract active buyers.
- Work with your agent to set expectations around typical days on market and months of inventory for your specific neighborhood and price band.

* * *

## Bottom Line

Portland is not one housing market. Buyers and sellers need to look at neighborhood, property type and price range to understand real leverage and timelines.
