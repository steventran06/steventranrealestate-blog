Greater Portland's single-family market is tighter than the condo market right now. Across the metro there are about 5, 941 active single-family listings and 2, 375 pending, which works out to roughly 2.5 months of inventory and an average of 43 days on market for sold homes. That mix points to a market where many detached homes are selling reasonably quickly and buyer activity is steady.

Condominiums are acting differently: the metro-wide condo inventory sits at about 7.1 months of inventory, with average days on market for sold condos around 87. In plain terms, condo shoppers generally see more choices and more time to evaluate listings compared with most detached-home buyers in the metro area.

## 🔥 Where Single-Family Homes Look Most Competitive

When single-family months of inventory is low and pending activity is relatively high, listings tend to move faster and face more competition. In parts of the city such as Northeast Portland (1.5 months of inventory, average days on market 28) and North Portland (1.6 months, 29 days), buyers should expect quicker turnarounds and more active bidding environments. That combination usually favors sellers and requires buyers to make decisions faster.

## 🏢 The Condo Market Is a Different Story

Condominiums across many parts of the metro are showing higher months of inventory and longer marketing times than detached homes. That means condo buyers often have more choices and more time to evaluate a property, while condo sellers generally need sharper pricing and marketing to stand out.

| Area | Single-Family Inventory | Condo Inventory | Inventory Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| North Portland | 1.6 months | 9.5 months | 7.9 months |
| Lake Oswego/West Linn | 3.6 months | 10.1 months | 6.5 months |
| Beaverton | 2.7 months | 9.1 months | 6.4 months |
| Northeast Portland | 1.5 months | 6.7 months | 5.2 months |
| West Portland | 3.3 months | 7.6 months | 4.3 months |

Look at North Portland for a clear contrast: single-family inventory is 1.6 months with 29 days on market, while condos show 9.5 months of inventory and 112 days on market. Similar gaps appear in Lake Oswego/West Linn, Beaverton and Northeast Portland. Condos tend to have lower pending-to-active ratios and longer days on market than single-family homes in many neighborhoods, so pricing and presentation matter more for condo sellers and condo buyers can take advantage of a slower pace.

## 💰 What This Could Mean for Buyers

- If you're shopping for a single-family home in Northeast, North or Southeast Portland, expect faster-moving listings and plan to act quickly when you find a good fit.
- Condo shoppers will generally find more inventory and more time to evaluate, places like North Portland, Lake Oswego/West Linn and Beaverton show especially long marketing windows.
- Markets with higher months of inventory, like Columbia and Yamhill counties, often give buyers more leverage and time for negotiating contingencies and inspections.
- In higher-priced pockets (Lake Oswego/West Linn, West Portland), inventory can be moderate while prices are high, so have financing and priorities lined up before writing an offer.

* * *

## 🏠 What This Could Mean for Sellers

- In the fastest single-family neighborhoods, prepare for quick showings and strong early interest; pricing and condition matter because homes are moving faster.
- Condo sellers should expect longer marketing times in many neighborhoods and focus on competitive pricing, clear photos and targeted marketing to reduce days on market.
- If you're selling in buyer-opportunity areas, build more time into your sale plan and be ready to adjust price or terms if your goal is a faster closing.
- For higher-end listings, highlight features and comparables clearly, these areas can have moderate inventory but buyers still compare carefully.

* * *

## Bottom Line

Portland is not one housing market. Buyers and sellers should look at neighborhood-level data, property type and price range to understand how these trends affect their decisions.

Market statistics are based on the weekly TMO Reports data used for this analysis and are subject to change. Real estate conditions can vary significantly by neighborhood, property type and price range.
