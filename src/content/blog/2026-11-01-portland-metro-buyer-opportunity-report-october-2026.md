This is a look back at October housing activity across Portland Metro. The goal is to point out signals from October that could matter for buyers as we move into November.

Only two weekly market snapshots were available for October, so the changes are modest. The broad takeaway: single-family listings were slightly higher in some areas while several condo markets showed smaller inventory shifts, and price reductions were common across many listings.

> **About this report:** Only 2 weekly market reports were available for October 2026, dated Oct 12 and Oct 19. This gives us a useful snapshot of buyer conditions, but it should not be treated as a complete picture of every week of the month.

* * *

## 🏡 Where Buyers Had More Options

Buyers had a bit more room in neighborhoods where listings rose or buyer activity eased. Notable examples include Yamhill County, where single-family listings increased, and parts of Greater Portland and NW Portland where more homes showed up for sale and competition eased slightly.

### Yamhill County

**433 active listings | about 71 days to sell**

Homes were also taking longer to sell than in many parts of the metro, which can give buyers more time to compare properties and potentially negotiate.

### Columbia County

Homes were also taking longer to sell than in many parts of the metro, which can give buyers more time to compare properties and potentially negotiate.

### Marion County

### Oregon City/Canby

Homes were taking a little longer to sell, which can reduce some of the pressure to make an immediate decision.

## 🔥 Where Homes Were Moving Faster

Some pockets still tightened up and required quicker decisions. North Portland single-family inventory stayed very low, and Lake Oswego/West Linn saw a drop in single-family listings while buyer activity rose, meaning buyers there had less time to consider offers.

**128 active listings | about 42 days to sell**

This area remained more competitive than the markets offering buyers the most time and choice.

### Milwaukie/Clackamas

## 🏢 Houses vs. Condos

Condo markets behaved differently across the region. In places like North Portland and West Portland there were more condo choices overall, while Beaverton condos saw fewer active listings. That means in some neighborhoods condo shoppers had more to pick from and more time, and in others condos moved faster.

For buyers, that means you need to treat condos as neighborhood-specific: some buildings or areas give more negotiating room, others remain more competitive than the rest of the metro area.

## 📈 What Changed During October?

With just two weekly snapshots, most changes were modest. Greater Portland single-family listings rose by about 43 units while the typical single-family pending activity eased a touch, suggesting slightly less competition overall for detached homes. Yamhill County and Beaverton showed noticeable increases in single-family supply, while Lake Oswego/West Linn saw a meaningful drop in available single-family homes. On the condo side, Greater Portland condos had fewer active listings overall but average condo sale prices edged up slightly, showing prices can still be steady even as choices shift.

- Lake Oswego/West Linn: there were 21 fewer active listings.

## 💰 Where Sellers Were Cutting Prices

Price reductions were widespread in October. We tracked 302 unique listings and 298 with known reductions, with a typical reduction near \$50,050 and a median reduction percent of about 6.2 percent. Reductions can mean sellers are motivated or that an original price was higher than the market supported. A price drop is a signal to look closer, but it does not automatically make a property a bargain.

## 💡 What This Could Mean If You're Buying in November

- In neighborhoods where listings rose, for example Yamhill County and parts of NW Portland, buyers often had more choices and slightly more time to compare homes. Use that to be selective and keep inspection and financing protections in place.
- Where inventory stayed very low, like North Portland and parts of Lake Oswego/West Linn, be prepared to move faster: have financing preapproval and a plan for earnest timelines.
- Watch price reductions. About 300 unique listings saw reductions in October with a typical cut around \$50k (roughly 6%). That can create negotiating opportunities, but always confirm condition and comparables.
- Condo markets vary by neighborhood. If you want a condo, expand your search to areas with higher condo inventory, but don’t assume every condo market is the same, check recent sale pace and active listings by neighborhood.

## 👀 What I'm Watching in November

- Whether the extra listings in Yamhill County and parts of NW Portland keep increasing or level off.
- If Lake Oswego/West Linn’s drop in available single-family homes continues and buyer activity remains higher there.
- How price reductions evolve, especially in neighborhoods with larger median cuts such as Hillsdale.

## Bottom Line

Portland Metro is made up of many different local markets. Opportunities and timing depend on location, property type, condition and price range, so local detail matters when you’re shopping.

Market statistics are based on the available weekly TMO Reports and historical price-drop reports analyzed for October 2026. Market conditions, listing availability and pricing are subject to change and can vary significantly by neighborhood, property type and price range.
