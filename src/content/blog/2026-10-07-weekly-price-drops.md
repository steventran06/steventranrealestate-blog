## What Buyers Should Know About Price Reductions

A price reduction is a data point, not a verdict. When evaluating a cut consider comparable sales in the neighborhood, how the home’s condition and deferred maintenance affect cost to bring it up to market, and results from professional inspections. Factor in your financing terms and appraisal sensitivity, and assess local demand and days on market. Combine the reduction amount with comps and inspection findings to judge whether the price aligns with value and risk.

A price reduction can indicate seller motivation, but it can also mean that the original asking price was higher than the market supported.

The reduction itself is only one piece of the puzzle. Buyers should still review comparable sales, property condition, disclosures, inspection findings, financing terms and neighborhood demand before deciding how much value a price change actually creates.
