Across Greater Portland the single-family market looks relatively tight: 2.7 months of inventory, a pending-to-active ratio near 27%, and homes that closed in about 43 days on average. That combination usually means buyers need to be prepared to move efficiently when they find the right house.

Condominiums are acting differently. As a group condos show about 7 months of inventory, a much lower pending-to-active ratio around 13%, and average days on market near 88. In many parts of the metro that translates to more choices and more time for buyers and sellers to negotiate.

## 🔥 Where Single-Family Homes Look Most Competitive

Northeast Portland (1.6 months), North Portland (1.7 months) and Southeast Portland (2.0 months) all combine low inventory, higher pending activity and short marketing times. When inventory is low and pending ratios are relatively high, listings move faster and buyers should expect quicker decision windows and more active competition.

## 🏢 The Condo Market Is a Different Story

Condos are behaving differently than detached homes in many neighborhoods. Several condo markets have four to six months of inventory among the 'hottest' condo areas, and some submarkets show seven to ten months. That usually means condo listings sit longer and see lower pending activity compared with single-family homes.

| Area | Single-Family Inventory | Condo Inventory | Inventory Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| North Portland | 1.7 months | 8.6 months | 6.9 months |
| Beaverton | 2.6 months | 9.3 months | 6.7 months |
| Lake Oswego/West Linn | 3.5 months | 10 months | 6.5 months |
| Northeast Portland | 1.6 months | 7.4 months | 5.8 months |
| West Portland | 3.3 months | 7.4 months | 4.1 months |

The condo versus single-family gap is wide in places like North Portland, where single-family inventory is 1.7 months versus condos at 8.6 months, and in Beaverton where single-family sits at 2.6 months versus condos at 9.3 months. Across multiple areas condos have lower pending-to-active ratios and much longer days on market, so buyers often have more negotiating time with condos while single-family buyers face faster timelines.

## 💰 What This Could Mean for Buyers

- If you’re shopping single-family in hot pockets like Northeast or North Portland, expect faster timelines: be pre-approved and ready to act when you find the right house.
- For condo shoppers, especially in markets with 7 to 10 months of inventory, plan for more options and more time to compare buildings, finishes and HOA rules.
- In county markets with higher inventory, you can typically take more time to run inspections and consider contingencies; still bring clear offers when the right property appears.
- Talk to your agent about realistic offer timing and escalation options for competitive neighborhoods, and about strategies that preserve your protections in slower markets.

* * *

## 🏠 What This Could Mean for Sellers

- Sellers in the fastest single-family pockets should price to market and be prepared for quick showings; good presentation matters because buyers will decide fast.
- If you’re selling a condo or in a higher-inventory county, plan for a longer marketing window and highlight updates and value drivers to stand out.
- Compare nearby closed sales and marketing times with your agent instead of relying on broad metro numbers; the condo vs single-family gap can change expectations for timing and pricing.
- Be realistic about contingency timing: faster-moving neighborhoods may demand quicker closing schedules, while slower markets allow more flexibility on settlement dates.

* * *

## Bottom Line

Portland is not one housing market. Results depend on neighborhood, property type and price band. Buyers and sellers should review the specific numbers for their area, and use those local indicators to set realistic timelines and expectations.
