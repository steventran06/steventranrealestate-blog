Greater Portland is showing two very different pictures depending on property type. As an aggregate, single-family homes sit at about 2.9 months of inventory with roughly 2, 123 pending listings and an average 44 days on market for sold homes. That combination points to a market where many detached homes are moving relatively quickly and selling in under two months on average.

Condominiums are behaving differently across the metro. The condo inventory sits around 7.3 months with about 180 pending listings and an average of 89 days on market for sold condos. That higher inventory and longer marketing time means condo buyers generally have more choices and more time to evaluate options than buyers of detached homes.

## 🔥 Where Single-Family Homes Look Most Competitive

Where single-family inventory is lowest, expect faster-moving listings and more buyer competition. Northeast Portland is the tightest single-family pocket with 1.6 months of inventory, a 32 pending-to-active ratio, and homes selling in about 32 days. North Portland and Southeast Portland are also fast-moving, with under 2.5 months of inventory and typical sold times in the mid-30s to high-30s for days on market.

## 🏢 The Condo Market Is a Different Story

Condos are behaving differently than detached homes across many parts of the metro. Multiple neighborhoods show 5 to nearly 10 months of condo inventory, and sold condos are taking considerably longer to move in some areas. That means condo shoppers will often see more active listings and longer decision windows compared with buyers focused on single-family homes.

| Area | Single-Family Inventory | Condo Inventory | Inventory Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Northeast Portland | 1.6 months | 8.5 months | 6.9 months |
| North Portland | 1.8 months | 7.9 months | 6.1 months |
| Lake Oswego/West Linn | 3.7 months | 9.6 months | 5.9 months |
| Beaverton | 2.6 months | 8.2 months | 5.6 months |
| Milwaukie/Clackamas | 2.8 months | 7.1 months | 4.3 months |

Across the metro the pattern is clear: single-family inventory is generally lower and pending activity higher, while condos typically have multiple months more inventory and longer days on market. For example, Northeast Portland single-family inventory is 1.6 months with a 32 pending ratio and 32 days on market, while Northeast condos have 8.5 months of inventory, a 10.1 pending ratio, and about 101 days on market. There are exceptions, such as Lake Oswego where condos show very high inventory but sold times that are closer to single-family pacing, so it pays to look at the specific neighborhood and price range.

## 💰 What This Could Mean for Buyers

- If you are shopping for a single-family home in Northeast, North or Southeast Portland, be prepared for faster timelines. Expect decisions and offers within a few weeks, given sold days on market in the 30s and under 2.5 months of inventory.
- Buyers looking for more selection and time should consider areas with higher months of inventory like Columbia County (5.2 months) or Yamhill County (4.3 months). Longer marketing times give more room to compare homes and plan inspections.
- Condo shoppers generally have more choices and longer search windows. In parts of the city condos are sitting on the market for two to three months or longer, which can mean more negotiating flexibility.
- Bring your financing in order and a clear timeline for inspections and closing, because the level of competition varies a lot by neighborhood and property type.

* * *

## 🏠 What This Could Mean for Sellers

- Sellers in the tight single-family pockets should expect faster activity. Pricing and presentation matter when homes are selling in about a month in areas like Northeast Portland.
- If you need to sell a condo or a home in a higher-inventory area, plan for a longer marketing period and work with your agent on competitive pricing and targeted improvements to stand out.
- High-priced submarkets such as Lake Oswego have different dynamics: even with more inventory, buyers are active at higher price points, so position your home based on local sold metrics rather than metro averages.

* * *

## Bottom Line

Portland is not one housing market. Single-family and condo behavior can be very different from neighborhood to neighborhood, so buyers and sellers should evaluate their specific area, property type and price range when making decisions.

Market statistics are based on the weekly TMO Reports data used for this analysis and are subject to change. Real estate conditions can vary significantly by neighborhood, property type and price range.
