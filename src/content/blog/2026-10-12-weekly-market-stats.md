Across Greater Portland the single-family market looks relatively tight: about 6, 057 active single-family listings, 1, 919 pending, and roughly 3.2 months of inventory. Average days on market for sold single-family homes sits near 48 days and the average sale price is about \$658, 143. Those numbers point to a market where many detached homes still move within a few weeks to a couple of months.

Condominiums are behaving differently. There are 1, 383 active condo listings in the metro with about 7.7 months of inventory and an average days-on-market for sold condos of 100 days. That combination means condo buyers generally see more choices and more time to make decisions, while condo sellers are often facing longer marketing periods.

## 🔥 Where Single-Family Homes Look Most Competitive

When inventory is low, pending activity is strong and days on market are short, you usually see more competition among buyers. Neighborhoods with the lowest months of inventory and the highest pending-to-active ratios are where offers tend to move fastest and sellers have more leverage. Examples in this report include North Portland (1.9 months) and Northeast Portland (2.0 months), both with active pending activity and sub-50 day average marketing times.

## 🏢 The Condo Market Is a Different Story

Condos are clearly different in many Portland neighborhoods. Metro-wide condos show about 7.7 months of inventory and an average of 100 days on market for sold units. Several condo submarkets have even longer selling timelines, meaning condo buyers often have more negotiating room, and condo sellers should expect longer marketing periods than for detached homes.

The difference between condos and single-family homes is particularly stark in some neighborhoods. In Northeast Portland single-family inventory is about 2 months with 33 days on market, while condos sit at 9.5 months and 113 days on market. North Portland shows a similar gap: about 1.9 months for detached homes versus 8.3 months for condos, with condos averaging more than 125 days on market. In most areas across the metro single-family listings move faster and face more active demand, while condos generally offer more choices and longer decision windows for buyers.

## 💰 What This Could Mean for Buyers

- If you’re shopping for a detached home in hotspots like Northeast, North or Southeast Portland, expect faster timelines and stronger competition; have financing and inspections lined up so you can move when the right home appears.
- Condo shoppers will usually find more inventory and longer marketing times; use that extra time to compare comparable units, review HOA docs and plan inspections.
- In higher-inventory areas (for example Columbia County and Yamhill County at about 4.8 months), buyers often have more room to negotiate timing and terms, but still confirm local conditions with an agent.
- Know the price bands: average single-family sale in the metro is about \$658, 143, while the average condo sale is about \$348, 337. Price expectations and competition can vary a lot by neighborhood and product type.

* * *

## 🏠 What This Could Mean for Sellers

- Sellers of detached homes in tight pockets should expect quicker showings and shorter marketing times; accurate pricing and strong presentation can capture that active demand.
- Condo sellers should plan for longer marketing periods, average days on market for condos is around 100 days, so factor time into your timing and pricing strategy.
- In slower single-family markets (areas with 4+ months of inventory), be prepared to allow more time for offers and consider updates or marketing that highlight value.
- Work with your agent to set realistic expectations based on property type, neighborhood and the local months of inventory rather than metro averages.

* * *

## Bottom Line

Portland is not one housing market. Conditions depend on neighborhood, property type and price range, so buyers and sellers should review the specific data for their area before making decisions.
