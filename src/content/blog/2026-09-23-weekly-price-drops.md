## What Buyers Should Know About Price Reductions

A price reduction is a prompt to dig deeper, not a conclusion. Compare recent nearby sales and adjust for size, lot and upgrades; account for deferred maintenance and repair costs found in inspections; confirm how financing terms and contingencies affect net price; and gauge current local demand and days on market. Use comps, a thorough inspection and clear financing plans to evaluate whether a reduction changes the property’s value to you.

A price reduction can indicate seller motivation, but it can also mean that the original asking price was higher than the market supported.

The reduction itself is only one piece of the puzzle. Buyers should still review comparable sales, property condition, disclosures, inspection findings, financing terms and neighborhood demand before deciding how much value a price change actually creates.
